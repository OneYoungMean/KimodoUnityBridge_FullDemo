# Kimodo Unity Bridge Known Issues

This is an informational record of observations from animation-agent tests. It is part of the skill's supporting material, not a mandatory workflow, scoring rubric, or set of hard thresholds. When one of these issues appears, continue with the task using the best available route and evidence; do not stop merely because the issue is listed here. Revisit this file when the execution loop, Pose test, command schema, or Unity/API integration is updated.

## Composite actions and pose/constraint selection

- A composite action may be generated as one long Clip even when approach and object interaction have different goals and acceptance evidence. An agent may only split the action after a failed attempt. The general Clip-boundary logic remains open for later workflow design.
- The priority between a FullBody Pose and end-effector constraints is not yet uniform for interaction actions. Some actions may work better from a verified starting Pose with an effector reaching the target while the rest of the body remains coordinated; this note does not impose a universal choice or numeric rule.
- The current scene Pose may be a T-pose, bind pose, or another state that is unsuitable as an animation start. A generation chain may accidentally reuse an unchecked scene Pose. Reliable Pose provenance and transfer between sequential Clips should be designed together with Pose test and continuation support.
- Reusing an unchecked starting Pose can carry foot-height or ground-contact errors into every dependent Clip. A floating-foot start may make the character appear to hop throughout the approach and interaction. The end of a verified preceding Clip is usually a better continuation source than repeatedly sampling the initial scene Pose; measure the actual pose and inspect foot contact before reuse.
- Agents can use analysis evidence, curve inspection/editing, and Unity APIs for local repair, but multi-attempt work may still split too late, transfer poses ambiguously, or stop before final Timeline/playback verification. Convergence and completion behavior should be improved together with the broader tool loop.

## Prop pickup handoff and visible verification

- Moving the tabletop prop itself with a Transform curve can be fragile when Timeline start-offset handling, Animator binding, or the prop's parent transform changes the effective curve. The saved curve may look plausible while the evaluated scene leaves the prop at its original parent origin or far from the hand. Inspect evaluated world transforms at the handoff frames and verify the relevant Timeline offset/binding behavior.
- For a simple visual pickup, a hand-attached visual proxy can be easier to control than moving the tabletop object across the scene: keep the original prop on the table until contact, then switch visibility to a matching proxy attached to the hand and align them at the handoff. This is a workflow option, not a universal requirement; avoid showing both copies at once, and preserve deterministic Timeline scrubbing/playback.
- A disabled or unconfigured runtime follow component does not establish that a prop track follows the character hand. Confirm the actual binding or attachment mechanism and sample the prop against the hand during the evaluated Timeline.
- Static samples, generation-analysis images, and Game View captures can disagree or omit the relevant objects. Agents have reported close hand-prop distances that could not be reproduced in the saved scene, and captures that showed neither the character nor the interaction. Keep claims tied to reproducible samples and images of the actual scene; if a capture is black, framed elsewhere, or otherwise inconclusive, treat visual completion as unknown and investigate the camera and render state.
- Scene framing is part of whether an interaction can be inspected. A valid animation can appear absent when the active camera faces away from the character or target. Check the intended view after integration rather than inferring visibility from object existence or animation curves alone.
- Changing Director end behavior or another isolated playback setting can leave the main interaction defect untouched. After a repair, re-evaluate the same relevant Timeline frames and inspect the visible handoff and ending in the intended scene view.
